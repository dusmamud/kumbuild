Your website is live. Now the work is getting customers to it. Nothing here costs money — it costs a little of your time, regularly.

## 1. Your Google Business Profile (Maps listing)

This is the single most important thing after launch. When someone nearby searches for what you do, your Maps listing is usually what they see first — before your website.

- **Claim it if you have not already**, and make sure the name, address, phone number and hours match your website exactly.
- **Add photos** — your shop front, inside, your work. Real photos, the same ones on your website.
- **Keep the hours current.** Wrong timings lose visits silently — a customer who arrives to a closed shop rarely comes back.
- **Reply to every review**, good or bad, in your own words. A reply shows future customers that a real person is behind the business.

We give you guidance for the Maps listing when your site launches — see [Get found on Google](/grow/get-found-on-google/).

## 2. Ask happy customers for Google reviews

A steady trickle of real reviews is the strongest local signal there is — stronger than anything we can do to the website itself.

- **Ask at the right moment** — right after a happy customer pays, or when they compliment your work.
- **Make it easy** — send them your website or Maps link on WhatsApp; one tap should get them to the review box.
- **Never buy reviews, and never write your own.** Fake reviews get removed and damage trust; a few honest ones beat dozens of suspicious ones.

## 3. Share your website everywhere you already talk to customers

- **WhatsApp** — send the link to regular customers, and put it in your WhatsApp Business profile if you use one.
- **Instagram and Facebook** — put the link in your bio, and share it when you post a new product, offer, or photo.
- **Offline** — print it on bills, visiting cards, and your shop board. Customers who have visited once are the easiest to bring back.

## 4. Keep the basics fresh

- Prices, timings and phone number current — on the website (message us, see [Changes after launch](/your-website/changes-after-launch/)) and on your Maps listing.
- If you add a service or change a price, tell us the same day. An out-of-date website quietly costs you customers.

## 5. Write about what customers ask you

Short, practical answers — what things cost, how to choose, what to expect — bring searchers to you before they are ready to buy. That is exactly what the [Kumbuild blog](https://blog.kumbuild.com) is for; start with [why your shop needs a website](https://blog.kumbuild.com/why-your-shop-needs-a-website/).

A new website takes days — sometimes a couple of weeks — to appear in search results. That is normal. If your site still does not appear for its exact business name after two weeks, see [Troubleshooting](/help/troubleshooting/).
